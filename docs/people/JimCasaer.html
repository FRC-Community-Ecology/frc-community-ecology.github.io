About Me

After graduating as a MSC in Forestry at the Faculty of Bioscience Engineering of the Katholieke Universiteit Leuven (KUL) in Belgium, I spent a year at the at the University of Natural Resources and Life Sciences in Vienna (DOKU - Austria), specialising in wildlife management and research techniques. Back in Belgium I made a PhD on the habitat use of roe deer.

Since 1999 I’ve been working at the Research Institute for Nature and Forests of the Flemish Government (INBO). Currently, research into the ecology and adaptive management of ungulate species such as wild boar and roe deer are at the centre of my work. For this purpose we combine different types of data and methods such as the analysis of hunting data, camera trapping, GPS-telemetry, drones, genetics, GIS and population modelling, but also social survey methods to investigate human attitudes and are in close contact with stakeholder groups, foresty administration and policy makers. Thereby, research into socio-ecological systems and decision science are becoming more prominent parts of my research activities to find answers for complex wildlife management issues and enhance human-wildlife coexistence in Flanders
